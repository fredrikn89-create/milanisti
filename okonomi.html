Financials

Overview

How much do Milan earn, who really owns the club, and how is it run? Here the finances are explained simply, with figures from the club's accounts.

In short

Milan earn more money than five years ago, but the season without Europe ended in a loss.

Revenue 2025/26

€464.6m

All the money the club brought in during the season that ended on 30 June 2026.

Result 2025/26

about −€24m

The club spent around 24 million more than it earned. The first loss since 2021/22.

Record revenue 2024/25

€494.5m

The highest revenue in the club's history, with the Champions League and big player sales.

Net debt at 30.06.2026

€145.3m

Loans minus cash. Up from about €92m the year before.

Equity at 30.06.2026

€176.4m

What the club owns, minus what it owes. Still well above zero.

Sponsorship revenue 2025/26

over €100m

For the first time in the club's history, according to the club.

In short: Milan are in much better health than five years ago. Back then the club was losing almost 100 million euros a year. From 2022/23 to 2024/25 the club made a small profit three years in a row.

Even so, the 2025/26 season showed how fragile the finances are. Without European football, Milan lost an estimated €70m to €80m in revenue, and the accounts ended with a loss of around €24m. Debt also rose, partly because the club used more of its credit lines.

Our verdict: the finances are stable, but not strong. The wage level is moderate compared with revenue, and the equity is solid. But the club depends on two things to make a profit: the Champions League and selling players at a profit. Without either of them, it quickly goes into the red.

Who owns Milan?

The American investment firm RedBird Capital Partners, led by Gerry Cardinale, is in control. Since January 2026, Elliott has been completely out.

Owner

RedBird Capital Partners

New York investment firm, founded and led by Gerry Cardinale.

Stake

99,93 %

The rest is owned by small shareholders.

Purchase price 2022

€1.2bn

The price RedBird paid Elliott for the club.

Vendor loan from Elliott

€550m (2022)

Elliott lent RedBird a large part of the purchase price. The loan has now been paid off.

New loan 2026

about €550m

Comvest Credit Partners provided the loan that replaced the Elliott loan in January 2026.

When RedBird bought Milan in 2022, it did not have all the money itself. So the seller, Elliott, lent RedBird around €550m of the purchase price. This is called a vendor loan. As security, Elliott held the shares in Milan as collateral. This means Elliott could have taken the club back if the loan was not repaid.

The loan had an interest rate of around 8% and was originally due on 31 August 2025. In December 2024, RedBird paid in €170m. This reduced the loan to around €489m and moved the due date to July 2028.

On 30 January 2026, RedBird announced that the Elliott loan had been refinanced. This means RedBird took out a new loan, arranged by Comvest Credit Partners, and used the money to pay off Elliott. Il Sole 24 Ore puts the amount at €550m, while Bloomberg reported around 700 million dollars. Elliott's representatives Gordon Singer and Dominic Mitchell left the board at the same time. The debt sits with the holding company, not with the club itself.

At the time of the 2022 purchase, it was reported that several investors had joined in with smaller stakes through RedBird: Yankees Global Enterprises (owners of the New York Yankees baseball team, around 10%), the Main Street Advisors fund (where LeBron James and Drake are passive investors) and the Italian Silva family. Yankees president Randy Levine sits on Milan's board.

  1. 1986

    Silvio Berlusconi / Fininvest

    Berlusconi bought a club with serious financial problems. The purchase was announced on 20 February 1986, and the price was around 15 billion lire. It was the start of the most successful era in the club's history.

  2. 2017

    Li Yonghong

    China's Li Yonghong bought 99.93% on 13 April 2017. The price was €740m, including €220m in debt. To manage it, he borrowed €303m from the Elliott fund, at an interest rate of 11.5%.

  3. 2018

    Elliott Management

    Li was unable to repay the loan. So Elliott took over the club in July 2018. Elliott sorted out the finances and appointed Paolo Scaroni as chairman.

  4. 2022

    RedBird Capital Partners

    RedBird bought the club from Elliott for €1.2bn. The deal was announced in June and completed on 31 August 2022. Elliott financed a large part of the purchase with a vendor loan.

  5. 2024

    Vendor loan changed

    RedBird paid in €170m in December 2024. The remaining loan of around €489m was given a new due date of July 2028.

  6. 2026

    Elliott completely out

    RedBird refinanced the loan through Comvest in January 2026. Elliott left the board, and RedBird is now the sole controlling owner.

San Siro is not owned by Milan alone. The stadium and the area around it were bought from the City of Milan for €197m on 5 November 2025. The buyer is the company Stadio San Siro S.p.A., which Milan and Inter own half each. Read more under «Stadium».

How the club is run

RedBird runs the club through the board, while a chief executive leads the day-to-day running.

Chairman

Paolo Scaroni

Has chaired the board since 2018.

Chief executive

Massimo Calvelli

CEO since 26 June 2026, after Giorgio Furlani was sacked.

Chief financial officer

Stefano Cocirio

CFO since February 2023 and a member of the board.

Sporting director

None

In June 2026, RedBird chose not to appoint a sporting director.

The board has nine members, including Gerry Cardinale, Paolo Scaroni, Massimo Calvelli and Yankees president Randy Levine. The board makes the big decisions, such as the budget, major purchases and hiring top executives.

Day-to-day running is led by chief executive Massimo Calvelli. Under him are, among others, chief financial officer Stefano Cocirio, chief revenue officer Maikel Oettle and chief brand officer Francesca Montini.

On the sporting side, Milan have no traditional sporting director since Igli Tare and Geoffrey Moncada had to leave in May 2026. Transfers are handled by Hendrik Almstadt, analysis by a dedicated football analytics department led by Bobby Gardiner, and Gerry Cardinale has the final say. Zlatan Ibrahimović is a senior adviser to RedBird. The head coach is Rúben Amorim.

Below the first team, the club has Milan Futuro, a development side for young players that plays in Serie D in 2026/27, coached by Jovan Kirovski. The Primavera (U20) and the academy are run by Vincenzo Vergine. Milan also have a women's team in Serie A Femminile.

The club owns its headquarters, Casa Milan, which was bought for €42m in 2020, and the Milanello training ground. San Siro is run together with Inter. Since November 2025, the clubs have owned the stadium through Stadio San Siro S.p.A. Milan have had the highest average attendance in Serie A two seasons in a row, over 72,000 per match in 2025/26.

We have not found the number of club employees in a reliable source.

San Siro and the new stadium

Milan and Inter now own San Siro together and are planning a new stadium next door.

Purchase price of San Siro

€197m

The stadium and the area around it, bought from the City of Milan on 5 November 2025.

Paid on signing

about €100m

First instalment and some additional items, according to Il Giorno. The rest will be paid later.

Project cost

about €1.2–1.3bn

The clubs' own estimate for the new stadium and site, not including demolition and clean-up.

Capacity

about 71,500

According to the plans submitted to the city council.

The buyer is Stadio San Siro S.p.A., which Milan and Inter own half each. The city council is contributing around €22m, including for clean-up work and moving a tunnel. Prosecutors opened an investigation into the sale on the same day the contract was signed. In October 2026, the administrative court (TAR) rejected the appeals against the sale.

The purchase is financed with loans. Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan lead the group of banks, together with the Italian banks Banco BPM and BPER Banca. Most of the new stadium will also be financed with loans, but it is unclear how much the clubs and owners will have to put in themselves.

The new stadium has been designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA. Construction is planned to start in the second half of 2027 and finish in 2031, well ahead of Euro 2032. The clubs will play at the current San Siro until then. After that, the old stadium will be demolished, apart from one section that will be kept. Gerry Cardinale has talked about 80,000 seats, but the plans show around 71,500.

Why does this matter for the finances? A modern stadium brings in much more from tickets, hospitality boxes, food and sponsors. Real Madrid increased their stadium revenue from €145m in 2019 to €248m after the redevelopment. Milan made €69.5m in matchday revenue in 2024/25.

Glossary

The key terms explained simply.

Revenue (income)
All the money the club brings in over a season, from tickets, TV, sponsors, prize money and player sales.
Costs
All the money the club spends, such as wages, travel, running costs and amortisation.
Wage bill / staff costs
Wages for all employees, including players and coaches, plus taxes and charges linked to wages.
Wage ratio
Wages divided by revenue. Shows how much of the revenue goes on wages.
Amortisation
A player's transfer fee is spread over the years of his contract. A €50m player on a five-year deal costs €10m a year in the accounts.
Write-down
An extra cost when a player or asset has become worth less than the accounts say.
Profit on player sales (plusvalenza)
The sale price minus what is still left to amortise on the player. If the club sells a player from its own academy, almost the whole sum is profit.
Operating result
Revenue minus costs from normal operations, before interest and tax.
Net result (profit or loss for the year)
What is left at the very end, after interest and tax. Plus means a profit, minus means a loss.
Debt
Money the club owes others, for example banks or other clubs.
Net debt
Loans minus the money the club has in the bank.
Equity
The value of everything the club owns minus everything it owes. Positive equity means the club owns more than it owes.
Vendor loan
When the seller lends the buyer part of the purchase price. Elliott did this when RedBird bought Milan.
Refinancing
Taking out a new loan to pay back an old one, often on different terms.
Credit line
An agreement with the bank that lets the club borrow up to a set amount when it needs to.
Squad cost rule
UEFA rule: wages, amortisation and agents' fees can make up at most 70% of revenue from 2025/26.
Football earnings rule
UEFA rule on how big a combined loss a club can have over three years.
Settlement agreement
An agreement between UEFA and a club that has broken the rules, with fines and targets the club must meet.
Deloitte Football Money League
Annual list of the clubs with the highest revenue in the world. Leaves out player sales.
