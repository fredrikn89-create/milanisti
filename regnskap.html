Financial Fair Play

UEFA has two main rules. A club cannot spend too much of its revenue on players, and it cannot lose too much money over three years. Here is Milan measured against both.

Rule 1: Squad cost

Player wages and amortisation as a percentage of revenue. The limit tightens from 90% in 2023 to 70% from 2025.

Rule 2: Losses over three years

UEFA's football earnings rule says a club can have a combined loss of up to €5m over three years; the limit can rise to €60m if the owners cover the loss with new money or equity, and to up to €90m for clubs in good financial health.

Each bar is the sum of the accounting result for the season and the two seasons before it, so it cannot be compared directly with the result for a single year. The numbers under each bar are the three seasons included (18 means 2017/18). The two dashed lines show the limits of −5 and −60 € million.

Milan broke the old Financial Fair Play rules for 2015–2018 and agreed to sit out the 2019/20 Europa League (confirmed by CAS on 28 June 2019); in 2022 the club signed a new settlement agreement with UEFA with a €2m fine and a conditional €13m fine; the agreement ended with the accounts at 30 June 2025, and according to Calcio e Finanza Milan met all the targets; when UEFA punished Fiorentina and Juventus, among others, in June 2026, Milan were not among the clubs sanctioned.

Our calculations are based on the club's accounts. UEFA uses its own rules, counts by calendar year, includes agent fees, and deducts spending on the academy, women's team and facilities, among other things. The official figures may therefore differ slightly.

From big losses to three years of small profits, and then back into the red.

The result is what is left after all revenue and costs, including interest and tax. Net debt is loans minus cash in the bank. In 2022/23 the club had more money in the bank than loans. Equity is the value of what the club owns minus what it owes.

The 2022/23 season brought a profit of €6.1m. It was the first time since 2006 that Milan had been in the black, which is why it was called the «first profit in 17 years». What made it special was that the profit came almost without selling players. It was down to the Champions League semi-final, bigger crowds and new sponsorship deals.

The profits in 2023/24 and 2024/25 were smaller, and they relied more on player sales. In 2024/25, profits from selling players accounted for €55.9m.

In 2025/26 things went wrong. Without European football, an estimated €70m to €80m in revenue disappeared. The club made a loss of around €24m. Net debt rose to €145.3m. According to the club, this was mostly because it used more of its credit lines for investments and projects, including the purchase of San Siro.

An important distinction: the loan RedBird took out to buy the club (the Comvest loan) sits in the holding company above Milan. It does not appear in the club's own accounts. But the owner has to pay interest and repayments on it, and that affects how much the owner can and will put into the club.

Staff costs are the wages of all employees, not just players. We worked out the percentage ourselves: staff costs divided by revenue. Many experts think under 60 to 70% is healthy. The detailed figures for 2025/26 have not been published yet.

Wages are the biggest cost. In 2024/25, all employees together cost €188.7m. Of this, around €160m went to players and coaches, according to Calcio e Finanza. By comparison, Inter spent €253m and Juventus €244m.

The second biggest item is amortisation. When Milan buy a player for €40m on a five-year contract, the full sum is not counted as a cost that year. Instead, the cost is spread out at €8m a year for five years. This is called amortisation. If the player becomes worth much less, the club may have to write down the value further. This is called a write-down.

Amortisation has risen sharply, from €71m in 2022/23 to €120m in 2024/25. This shows that Milan have bought many expensive players in recent years. Il Sole 24 Ore wrote that the club invested over €250m in players in 2023/24 and 2024/25.

Other big items in 2024/25 were services such as travel, advertising and consultancy (€91.4m), equipment and goods (€22.8m) and rent (€15.8m).

The rules on club finances

UEFA and the Italian federation set limits on how much clubs can spend.

UEFA has two main rules. The first is the squad cost rule. It says a club cannot spend more than 70% of its revenue on player wages, coaching staff wages, amortisation of transfer fees and agents' fees. The limit was 90% in 2023/24, 80% in 2024/25 and 70% from 2025/26. Revenue from player sales counts.

The second is the football earnings rule. A club can have a combined loss of €5m over three years. If the owners cover the loss with new money, the limit can rise to €60m. Clubs in good financial health can get up to €90m.

If a club breaks the rules, UEFA can hand out fines, limit its squad in Europe or, in the worst case, ban the club. UEFA and the club often agree a settlement agreement, where the club promises to meet certain targets by a deadline.

  1. 2019

    Banned from the Europa League

    Milan broke UEFA's old rules (Financial Fair Play) in the years 2015 to 2018. The club agreed to sit out the 2019/20 Europa League. The agreement was confirmed by the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) on 28 June 2019.

  2. 2022

    New settlement agreement with UEFA

    Milan broke the rules for the period up to 2021/22. The club received a €2m fine and a conditional fine of €13m that would only be paid if the club did not meet its targets.

  3. 2025

    Out of the agreement

    The agreement ended with the accounts to 30 June 2025. According to Calcio e Finanza, Milan met all the targets. The squad cost ratio was estimated at under 50% in 2023 and around 57% in 2024.

  4. 2026

    No sanction

    UEFA punished Fiorentina and Juventus in June 2026. Milan were not among the clubs punished.

In Italy there is also a separate rule from the Italian federation FIGC: the «costo del lavoro allargato». It compares wages and transfer costs with revenue. The limit was 0.8 and is being lowered to 0.7 from the 2026 summer window. If a club is over the limit, it cannot buy players until it has sold enough or the owners have put in money.

The 2025/26 loss is within UEFA's limits, because Milan made a profit in the years before. But another year of losses would leave less room to manoeuvre.
