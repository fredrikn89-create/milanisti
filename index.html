By Milanisti

74 million for a striker, nothing for a sporting director

Milan sold Rafael Leão, spent a club record on Gonçalo Ramos and still have no sporting director. It is time Gerry Cardinale admitted that the model is not working.

In June RedBird decided that Milan did not need a sporting director. Transfers would be run by Hendrik Almstadt, analysis by a dedicated “Football Intelligence” unit, and Gerry Cardinale would have the final say himself. First choice Markus Krösche had, according to Matteo Moretto, said yes, but Eintracht Frankfurt said no.

The summer that followed says a lot about why that was a problem. At the start of August Milan had still not sold a single player, and Libero blamed an inexperienced new structure. Then everything happened at once in the final week of the window.

Rafael Leão was sold to Galatasaray for €38 million on 30 August. Christopher Nkunku, Santiago Giménez and Samuele Ricci were loaned out with options to buy. In came Gonçalo Ramos from PSG for €74 million plus bonuses, a new club record, and Diego Moreira for €50 million.

Moreira has been a success. Ramos has not. He scored against Venezia in August and has not had a single shot on target in the four matches since. He scored for Portugal against Norway on 4 October, which only makes it clearer that the problem lies in how Milan use him. A €74 million striker needs a team built to get him the ball in the box.

That is exactly the bigger picture a sporting director is supposed to take care of. Someone has to say that a record-signing striker and a coach who plays two number tens behind a lone striker must fit together, and that there must be a plan B on the left when Saelemaekers and Estupiñán get injured in the same week.

According to Fantacalcio, Cardinale now wants a sporting director alongside Amorim after all. Krösche is mentioned again, along with José Boto, who worked with Amorim at Benfica. The club denies it. Zlatan Ibrahimović meanwhile turned up at Milanello for the first time in half a year.

There is no shame in changing course. What is shameful is spending over €120 million on two players without anyone at the club whose main job is to think about the sporting big picture. Milan are fifth, two points behind Roma, and unbeaten in the league. That is good enough in October. In January, someone has to own the window.

Published Wednesday 7 October 2026

By Milanisti

Cardinale: “We will build a stadium for 80,000”

Milan owner Gerry Cardinale talked about San Siro and an NBA arena at a conference in Los Angeles. In the same week, the administrative court cleared the way for the sale of the stadium.

Cardinale was one of the speakers at Bloomberg Screentime in Los Angeles on 1 October. The conference was mostly about media, but the RedBird owner also spent some time on Milan. His remarks only became known in Italy this week, reported by MilanNews, TuttoMercatoWeb and SempreMilan among others.

“We bought San Siro, the iconic stadium in Milan, and we'll build another stadium with 80,000 spectators,” Cardinale said. “And then we hope to bring the NBA team to Milan, build an NBA arena.” He described the plan as a Milanese sports and entertainment platform, and called Milan “the New York Yankees of Italian soccer”.

The figure of 80,000 is Cardinale's own. The plans submitted to the city show a stadium of around 71,500 seats, designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA. Nor was it RedBird alone that bought it. San Siro and the surrounding area were sold for €197 million in November 2025 to Stadio San Siro S.p.A., which Milan and Inter own half each.

On Tuesday 6 October the regional administrative court, TAR Lombardia, rejected all five appeals against the sale. Four were declared inadmissible and one was rejected on the merits. According to Il Giorno, the Sì Meazza committee will appeal to the Consiglio di Stato, and a separate criminal investigation into the sale is still ongoing.

The next step is the first renderings of the new stadium, expected in late October or early November. The deadline for comments on the environmental assessment is 1 November. Construction is scheduled to start in the second half of 2027 and finish in 2031, ahead of Euro 2032. Milan and Inter will keep playing at the current San Siro in the meantime, and parts of the old stadium will be preserved.

By Milanisti

Moreira is too good to sit on the bench

Three goals in the last two league matches, all as a substitute. Diego Moreira was Milan's best player in September, and now Rúben Amorim must dare to start him.

It is easy to forget that Diego Moreira cost €50 million. He was unveiled on 19 August, a few days before the season started, and was somewhat overshadowed by Gonçalo Ramos and the record fee paid to PSG. A month later, the Belgian from Strasbourg is the one everyone is talking about.

The fans have already made up their minds. Moreira won the September player of the month vote with around half the votes, ahead of Alphadjo Cissé and Adrien Rabiot. It is not hard to see why.

At the Olimpico Milan were 0–2 down at half-time and looked like a team without a plan. Moreira came on, volleyed home a cross after 65 minutes and fired in the equaliser from the edge of the box two minutes later. Against Lecce he needed four minutes on the pitch before he won the ball, went past Siebert and put away the 3–0. He is the youngest Milan player since 2004–05 to score two goals as a substitute in a Serie A match.

Yet he has started only one league match, and it went badly. In Turin he was used as right wing-back, with more defensive work than space to attack. He never got into the match and was substituted after an hour. Against Benfica he was on the bench from the start again.

That is the problem. Amorim has used Moreira as a second-half weapon, when the opposition is tired and the game opens up. It works, but it is a luxury Milan can no longer afford. Alexis Saelemaekers needs ankle surgery and will be out for months. Pervis Estupiñán injured his knee with Ecuador and is doubtful for Sassuolo.

The obvious solution is to give Moreira the left flank from the start, ideally in the attacking set-up with Chukwueze and Pulisic behind Ramos that Gazzetta has written about. He is at his best when he can cut inside onto his right foot, not when he has to run the line up and down in a back five.

There is a risk. Moreira is 22, and nobody knows whether he can handle 90 minutes every week with matches on both Sunday and Thursday. But a coach who asks for patience and says the team is “far from its potential” cannot at the same time leave his most effective player sitting and waiting.

Away at Sassuolo on Sunday is the perfect place to start.

Saelemaekers out for months, Estupiñán doubtful

Alexis Saelemaekers is likely to need surgery on the same ankle that was operated on in 2024. Pervis Estupiñán injured his knee with Ecuador. Both are left-sided options Milan are missing before Sassuolo.

Saelemaekers twisted his right ankle in training at Milanello at the end of September. The first scans showed no damage to bone or ligaments, but the pain returned when his training load increased. He was examined again at the weekend.

“Specialist tests showed that, following the recent training injury, the ankle has lost stability at the site of the previous surgery,” Milan said in a statement on Monday. The ankle was operated on in 2024, while he was playing for Roma, after a fracture of the medial malleolus. The club said a specialist evaluation is under way to decide on the right treatment.

According to Gazzetta dello Sport and Corriere dello Sport he will have surgery, but no date has been confirmed. Both expect him to miss the rest of 2026. Estimates range from more than three months to a return in February or March. “Now it's head down, work and patience. I'll come back stronger,” Saelemaekers wrote on Tuesday.

Estupiñán injured his knee in Ecuador's friendly against Japan in Yokohama on 1 October. The Ecuadorian federation reported on Monday a knee sprain with “internal involvement”, and he left the squad before the match against Panama. Italian media report a mild strain of the medial collateral ligament, but the club has not confirmed this. Reports differ on which knee it is.

No timeframe has been given. Several papers rule him out of Sunday's match at Sassuolo, while others call him doubtful. Either way, Davide Bartesaghi is the natural alternative on the left. It is also unclear whether Estupiñán will be fit for the Europa League match against Salzburg on 15 October.

By Milanisti

We celebrated 100 years of San Siro. Now it is to be demolished

Milan marked the stadium's centenary with a light show and a DJ on the roof. At the same time, the club is working to demolish it. It is hard to imagine a more self-contradictory anniversary week.

San Siro opened on 19 September 1926 with a derby. Inter won 6–3. Milan celebrated the day after, before the match against Lecce, with a ceremony and DJ Dardust on the stadium roof. The team responded with a 3–0 win. It was a fine evening.

It came after a humiliating Wednesday. Benfica won 2–0 at San Siro in the week the stadium turned 100, and the Mexican newspaper Récord wrote that Benfica had ruined the centenary. That is not entirely unfair.

But the biggest problem with the anniversary is not the result against Benfica. It is that Milan and Inter have bought the stadium from the city council for €197 million in order to demolish it. The new stadium is to be designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA and hold around 71,500 spectators. Only part of the Curva Sud will be preserved. The target is Euro 2032.

The matter is far from settled. Five appeals against the sale are pending at the regional administrative court (TAR), and Milan prosecutors have opened an investigation. The demolition alone is estimated to cost more than €115 million.

We understand the arguments. A modern stadium brings in the revenue Milan need to compete with the best in Europe. But a club that uses its centenary to sell nostalgia should also be honest about what it is planning.

That applies especially this year. Franco Baresi died on 31 July, aged 66. Milan are playing the whole season with “6 PER SEMPRE” on their shirts, and according to Gazzetta the club is considering naming the Curva Sud or the new stadium after him. No decision has been made.

Here is a suggestion: if the Curva Sud is to be preserved, let it bear Baresi's name. Then the new stadium will at least have one part that remembers where it comes from.

Published Monday 21 September 2026

By Milanisti

Amorim learned from Manchester. Now he has to learn from Benfica

The coach switched to a back four against Lecce and got his best performance. But the rotation against Benfica showed that he is still experimenting when he cannot afford to lose.

Rúben Amorim came to Milan with a reputation from Manchester United: a coach who would rather lose with his system than win without it. That is why Sunday against Lecce was more interesting than the 3–0 win itself.

For the first time this season Milan defended with a back four. Mario Gila moved out to right-back, and the team switched between 4-4-2 and 4-2-3-1. Amorim said it himself after the match: “What I felt in the last few matches is the same as what I felt at Manchester United, and I cannot make the same mistake.”

It is an admission that deserves respect. Milan have been weak in the first half in almost every match. Against Juventus the first shot on target came after 68 minutes. At the Olimpico the team were 0–2 down at half-time. A system that only works after three substitutions does not work.

But the praise stops there. Four days before Lecce, Amorim dropped five regular starters against Benfica, explaining that he has to rotate, “especially with a 40-year-old player”. Modrić was on the bench, Hutchinson got his first start, and Filippo Terracciano played centre-back in a European tie. The result was Benfica's first ever win over Milan and boos from San Siro both at half-time and after the final whistle.

After the match Amorim said the players were nervous, “the three defenders too”. That is a strange thing to say about a back line he had put together himself for the first time.

Rotation is necessary with nine matches in under a month. But there is a difference between resting two players and replacing half the spine of the team. When Amorim also says that “every week there will be a new starting XI”, it is no wonder Matteo Moretto reports questions in the dressing room and a less than ideal relationship with some of the older players.

Amorim is unbeaten in his first five league matches, something no Milan coach has managed since 1979–80. That is a good start. But next time it is the Europa League, against Salzburg on 15 October, he should field a team the players recognise. Flexibility is a strength. Experiments in matches that count are not.

Published Sunday 20 September 2026

By Milanisti

Pulisic, Rabiot and Moreira secured a comfortable win

Milan beat Lecce 3–0 and go into the international break in fifth place with eleven points. After a tough week, this was the most complete performance under Rúben Amorim so far.

After the defeat to Benfica on Wednesday, Amorim went back to his strongest team, and he made an important tactical change. Milan defended with a back four, with Mario Gila at right-back, instead of the three centre-backs the team have used all season. Modrić, Rabiot and Estupiñán came back in, and Pulisic started.

Christian Pulisic opened the scoring after 14 minutes. Strahinja Pavlović played the ball over the Lecce defence from his own half, and the American placed it calmly into the far corner past Wladimiro Falcone. Three minutes earlier Falcone had stopped him from almost the same spot. It was his first goal for Milan since December 2025.

Milan had the ball almost all the time in the first half-hour and finished with 69 per cent possession. Lecce created one good chance before the break, but Mike Maignan was equal to it.

After 56 minutes Pavlović thought he had headed in 2–0 himself, but VAR ruled the goal out for offside. Five minutes later it came anyway. Adrien Rabiot played a one-two with Gonçalo Ramos and made it 2–0. It was the Frenchman's second league goal of the season.

Lecce had their best chance after 65 minutes, but Pervis Estupiñán cleared off the line. The Ecuadorian was nevertheless whistled by parts of San Siro when he was substituted, which seems unfair after a match without any big mistakes.

Diego Moreira came on after 69 minutes and needed only four minutes. He won the ball, cut inside past Siebert and made it 3–0. Moreira has now scored three goals in two league matches, all as a substitute.

It was Milan's sixth straight win over Lecce in Serie A. The next match is away to Sassuolo on Sunday 11 October, after the international break.

Match report · Europa League · matchday 1

Published Wednesday 16 September 2026

By Milanisti

Benfica spoil the European party at San Siro

Milan lost 0–2 to Benfica in their first Europa League match. It was Benfica's first ever win over Milan, and San Siro booed Rúben Amorim.

Amorim made many changes from the Lazio match. Filippo Terracciano started in defence, Ardon Jashari and Yunus Musah in midfield, Davide Bartesaghi on the left and Omari Hutchinson got his first start. Modrić, Estupiñán and Loftus-Cheek sat on the bench for the whole match.

Benfica took the lead after 16 minutes. Dodi Lukébakio was given time outside the box and shot into the net, after build-up play from Jakub Kamiński. Eight minutes later Georgiy Sudakov hit the post for the visitors.

Milan got into the match before the break. Anatoliy Trubin saved from Terracciano, and Jashari hit the crossbar. Amorim brought on Gila and Pulisic for the second half, but it was Benfica who scored. After Koni De Winter had missed a big chance from close range, Souffian El Karouani delivered a cross that Kamiński turned in at the far post to make it 0–2.

Milan had 60 per cent possession, 17 shots and ten corners, but only four shots on target. Trubin saved twice from Pulisic, the second at full stretch nine minutes from time. It was too little, too late.

Benfica are now coached by Marco Silva, who took over from José Mourinho. For Amorim, who himself had success in Portugal with Sporting, the defeat was especially bitter. It was Benfica's first win over Milan at the seventh attempt, and it came in the week San Siro turns 100.

The next Europa League match is away to Salzburg on Thursday 15 October. First up is Lecce at home on Sunday.

Europa League kicks off with an old final rematch

Gatti snatched victory from Milan in stoppage time

Milan were two minutes from victory in Turin, but Federico Gatti headed in to make it 1–1. With one shot on target all match, the point was nonetheless not undeserved.

Juventus were clearly the better side in the first half. Mike Maignan punched away an early shot from Nicolás González and saved twice from Francisco Conceição before the break. Milan had almost as much of the ball, but never got past Bremer and Lucumí.

After the break Juventus hit the post on a counter-attack, and Amorim reacted after an hour. Chukwueze, Loftus-Cheek and Alphadjo Cissé came on for Moreira, Rabiot and Saelemaekers.

The changes worked quickly. After 68 minutes Chukwueze hit a long diagonal across the pitch, and Cissé brought the ball down and placed it in the far corner past Michele Vicario. It was Milan's first shot on target.

Milan defended deep for the rest of the match, and it very nearly held. Woltemade shot over five minutes from time. In the 92nd minute substitute Teun Koopmeiners swung in a cross, and Federico Gatti, who had also come off the bench, headed in to make it 1–1.

The numbers are clear. Juventus had 14 shots to Milan's three, five on target to one, and seven corners to one. Milan picked up three yellow cards, Estupiñán and De Winter among them. The point keeps the team unbeaten, but Amorim must find a way to create chances before the trip to Lazio on Saturday.
