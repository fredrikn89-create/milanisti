Management

From ownership and board to executive management and administration.

Owner

RedBird Capital Partners (Gerry Cardinale) · since 31 August 2022

The American investment firm RedBird Capital Partners, led by Gerry Cardinale, owns 99.93% of the club after buying it from Elliott in 2022 (approx. €1.2 billion). In January 2026 RedBird refinanced Elliott's vendor loan through Comvest, and Elliott thereby left the club entirely.

Ownership history

  1. 1986–2017

    Silvio Berlusconi / Fininvest

    Berlusconi bought the club on 20 February 1986 and saved it from bankruptcy; the most successful era in the club's history.

  2. 2017–2018

    Li Yonghong (Rossoneri Sport Investment Lux)

    The purchase of 99.93% was completed on 13 April 2017, partly financed by a €303 million loan from Elliott.

  3. 2018–2022

    Elliott Management

    Elliott took over in July 2018 after Li defaulted on the loan, and appointed Paolo Scaroni as chairman.

  4. 2022–

    RedBird Capital Partners

    Agreement on 1 June 2022, completed on 31 August 2022; Elliott initially kept a vendor loan/minority interest, which was refinanced and repaid in January 2026.

Facilities

  • Casa MilanThe club's headquarters on Via Aldo Rossi in the Portello area of Milan, opened in 2014, with a museum (Mondo Milan), club shop, ticket office, restaurant and media studio. The club bought the building for €42 million in 2020 after renting it from Vittoria Assicurazioni.
  • MilanelloThe training centre in Carnago (province of Varese), about 40 km north-west of Milan, opened in 1963 and covers around 160,000 m². It has several grass and artificial pitches, a medical centre and accommodation for some academy players.
  • San Siro (Stadio Giuseppe Meazza)Milan and Inter bought San Siro and the surrounding area from the City of Milan through the company Stadio San Siro S.p.A.; the deed was signed on 5 November 2025, just as prosecutors opened an investigation into the sale. The clubs are planning a new stadium designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA, financed with Goldman Sachs and J.P. Morgan as arrangers.
