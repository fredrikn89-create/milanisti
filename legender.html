    Background

    Franco Baresi grew up on a farm near Travagliato in Lombardy and reportedly had not watched football on TV until he was around ten years old. His older brother Giuseppe played as a defender for Inter, and both brothers had trials at Inter. The club chose Giuseppe and sent Franco home.

    Franco went to Milan instead, where he joined the youth side in 1972 and made his Serie A debut on 23 April 1978, aged 17. It was the start of a career in which he played only for Milan, over 20 seasons from 1977/78 to 1996/97.

    Baresi played as a libero and was known for reading the game, for staying tight behind the rest of the defensive line and for leading his team-mates. He took over the captain's armband at 22 and kept it for around fifteen seasons.

    Career

    Baresi was part of Milan's first scudetto in ten years in 1978/79, as a teenager. Milan were then relegated to Serie B twice in his first years at the club, and he won Serie B with the team in 1980/81 and 1982/83. In 1982 he became captain when Maldera and Collovati left the club.

    After Silvio Berlusconi took over and Arrigo Sacchi arrived, Milan became one of Europe's best teams. Baresi won the scudetto in 1987/88, and the European Cup (now the Champions League) in 1988/89 and 1989/90. He also won the Intercontinental Cup in 1989 and 1990 and the UEFA Super Cup in 1990. In 1989 he finished second in the Ballon d'Or voting, behind team-mate Marco van Basten.

    In the 1990s came the scudetto in 1991/92, 1992/93, 1993/94 and 1995/96, the Champions League in 1993/94 and the UEFA Super Cup in 1994. In total he won six league titles, three Champions League titles and four Italian Super Cups with Milan. He played his last season in 1996/97, and Milan have retired the number 6 shirt.

    For Italy he played 81 internationals and scored one goal. He was in the squad that became world champions in 1982, but did not play in the tournament. As captain at the 1994 World Cup he injured his meniscus but was back for the final against Brazil and missed his penalty in the shoot-out, which Italy lost.

    After his playing career he was briefly sporting director at Fulham in 2002. He then coached Milan's Primavera side (2002–2006) and the Berretti team until 2008. Later he held roles at the club, including as ambassador from 2017 and honorary vice-president from 2020. He was inducted into the Italian Football Hall of Fame in 2013. Franco Baresi died on 31 July 2026, aged 66.

    Background

    Paolo Maldini was born in Milan and is the son of Cesare Maldini, who captained both Milan and Italy and later coached his son at youth level, with the national team and at Milan. He joined Milan's youth department and spent his entire career at the club.

    He made his Serie A debut on 20 January 1985, aged 16, and established himself as a right-back the following season. Later he was used mostly as a left-back, and at the end of his career also as a centre-back.

    Maldini is known for calm, anticipation and good positioning, and for defending without relying on hard tackles. He played 25 seasons for Milan and retired in 2009, aged 40.

    Career

    Maldini won Serie A seven times with Milan and the Coppa Italia in 2002/03. In total there were five European Cup and Champions League titles, five UEFA Super Cup titles and two Intercontinental Cups.

    After Franco Baresi and Mauro Tassotti retired after 1996/97, Maldini took over the captain's armband at Milan. As captain he lifted the Champions League trophy in 2007, aged 38, becoming the oldest captain to have won the tournament. That same year Milan also won the Club World Cup.

    He finished third in the Ballon d'Or in 1994 and 2003 and runner-up in FIFA's player of the year award in 1995. He was named Serie A Defender of the Year in 2004. He played his last match for Milan on 31 May 2009, a 2–0 win over Fiorentina, and shirt number 3 was retired.

    For Italy he played 126 internationals and scored seven goals between 1988 and 2002, and was captain for about eight years. He played in the 1994 World Cup final and retired from international football on 18 June 2002.

    Maldini returned to Milan in 2018 as sporting director and became technical director in 2019. His contract ended in June 2023 after disagreement over the club's direction. He is also a co-owner of Miami FC.

    Background

    Alessandro Nesta was born in Rome. A Roma scout noticed him as a youngster, but his father, a Lazio supporter, turned the offer down. Nesta joined Lazio's youth section in 1985. He had played as a striker and midfielder before being moved to defence.

    He made his debut for Lazio in March 1994 and became captain in 1997. With Lazio he won the Coppa Italia in 1998, the Cup Winners' Cup in 1998/99, the UEFA Super Cup in 1999 and the scudetto and Coppa Italia double in 1999/2000. He played 261 matches in total for Lazio.

    As a centre-back he was known for technique, timing and reading of the game. He was named Serie A defender of the year four years in a row, from 2000 to 2003.

    Career

    Nesta moved from Lazio to Milan in 2002 and played there for ten seasons. He won the Champions League and Coppa Italia in 2002/03, his first season at the club, and the UEFA Super Cup in 2003.

    The scudetto came in 2003/04, and in 2004 Milan won the Supercoppa Italiana. He was also part of the side when Milan won the Champions League in 2006/07, followed by the UEFA Super Cup and the Club World Cup in 2007.

    He won the scudetto again in 2010/11 and the Supercoppa Italiana in 2011. For Milan he played 326 matches and scored 10 goals in all competitions, of which 224 matches and 7 goals in Serie A. He wore the number 13 shirt.

    For Italy he played 78 internationals without scoring, from 1996 to 2006. He helped win the 2006 World Cup and lost the Euro 2000 final. He was inducted into the Italian Football Hall of Fame in 2021.

    After Milan, Nesta played for Montreal Impact in MLS (2012–2013) and briefly for Chennaiyin in India in 2014. As a coach he has led Miami FC (2015–2017), Perugia, Frosinone, Reggiana and Monza. In June 2026 he was presented as coach of Avellino.

    Background

    Frank Rijkaard was born in Amsterdam. His father Herman was Surinamese and came to the Netherlands as a footballer; his mother Neel is Dutch. He grew up in the city and played football with Ruud Gullit. His youth years were spent at SC Buitenveldert, Blauw-Wit and DWS before he joined Ajax in 1979/80.

    He made his debut for Ajax on 23 August 1980, aged 17, and won the Eredivisie in 1981/82, 1982/83 and 1984/85, three KNVB Cups and the 1986/87 Cup Winners' Cup. He played over 200 league matches for Ajax in his first spell.

    Rijkaard was signed by Sporting CP in 1987, but could not play for the club and was loaned out to Real Zaragoza. Milan signed him after the season in Spain.

    Career

    Rijkaard came to Milan in 1988 and, together with Ruud Gullit and Marco van Basten, formed a Dutch trio under Arrigo Sacchi. He won the Supercoppa Italiana in 1988, the European Cup (now the Champions League) in 1988/89 and 1989/90, and in both years the Intercontinental Cup and the UEFA Super Cup (1989 and 1990).

    In the European Cup final in Vienna in 1990 he scored the only goal in the 1–0 win over Benfica. He was named best player in the Intercontinental Cup in Tokyo the same year.

    Milan won Serie A in 1991/92 and 1992/93, and Rijkaard was named Serie A player of the year (Guerin d'Oro) in 1992. The team also reached the 1992/93 Champions League final, which Milan lost. He left the club in 1993 after 201 matches and 26 goals, and returned to Ajax.

    For the Netherlands he played 73 internationals and scored 10 goals between 1981 and 1994. He won Euro 1988 and finished third at Euro 1992, and played at the 1990 and 1994 World Cups. He won the Champions League with Ajax in 1994/95.

    As a manager he led the Netherlands (1998–2000), Sparta Rotterdam, Barcelona (2003–2008), Galatasaray and Saudi Arabia. With Barcelona he won La Liga in 2004/05 and 2005/06 and the Champions League in 2005/06.

    Background

    Andrea Pirlo grew up in Flero in the province of Brescia and joined Brescia's youth section in 1992 after playing for local clubs. At 16 he made his Serie A debut for Brescia in 1995, and two years later he helped win the Serie B title. At that time he played as an attacking midfielder.

    In 1998 he joined Inter, but got few chances in the first team and was loaned out to Reggina for the 1999–2000 season. In the second half of 2000–01 he was back at Brescia on loan. There, manager Carlo Mazzone for the first time placed him as a deep-lying playmaker in front of the defence, a role that would shape the rest of his career.

    In the summer of 2001 Milan bought him from Inter, in a deal in which Dražen Brnčić went the other way. Pirlo made his Milan debut on 20 September 2001 in a UEFA Cup match against BATE Borisov. He was known for his vision, long passes, technique, composure on the ball and his free kicks, but not for pace, stamina or tackling. At Milan he got the nickname "the metronome" because he set the tempo of the team.

    Career

    In his first seasons at Milan, Pirlo played in a more attacking role. After injuries to Gattuso and Ambrosini, with coach Carlo Ancelotti's support he was allowed to drop back into the playmaker role in front of the defence. From 2002–03 this was his regular place in Milan's 4-3-2-1 system.

    2002–03 was a big season: Milan won the Champions League and the Coppa Italia, and the UEFA Super Cup followed in 2003. Pirlo was also part of the Serie A title in 2003–04 and the Supercoppa Italiana in 2004. In 2004–05 and 2005–06 Milan finished second in Serie A. The team lost the Champions League final in 2005 but won it in 2007.

    In 2007 Milan also took the UEFA Super Cup and the FIFA Club World Cup. Pirlo left the club in 2011, after Milan had won Serie A 2010–11. In total he played 401 matches and scored 41 goals for Milan in all competitions, and joined Juventus in May 2011.

    For Italy Pirlo played 116 internationals and scored 13 goals. He became a world champion in 2006 and was named in the tournament's team, and he won three man-of-the-match awards at Euro 2012. After Juventus (three league titles) and New York City FC he retired in November 2017. He coached Juventus (2020–21), Fatih Karagümrük (2022–23) and Sampdoria (2023–24), where he was sacked in August 2024.

    Background

    Gennaro «Rino» Gattuso is from Corigliano Calabro in the province of Cosenza. He came through Perugia's youth section from 1990 to 1995 and won two Primavera titles with the club before making his Serie A debut. He played ten matches without scoring for Perugia.

    In 1997, aged 19, he went to Glasgow Rangers in a transfer move that has been described as controversial. In October 1998 he moved on to Salernitana, and in the summer of 1999 Milan bought him. He was given the number 8 shirt, and in his debut season 1999–2000 he played 22 matches and scored one goal.

    Gattuso was primarily a defensive or central midfielder, occasionally used on the right. He was known for physical strength, hard work and tough tackling, but also had good positional sense and a powerful shot. He earned the nickname «Ringhio» (growl) for his fighting spirit. He formed a famous midfield pairing with Andrea Pirlo.

    Career

    Gattuso scored his first Milan goal in 2000, in a 3–2 win over Bologna. He quickly became a regular in midfield and played 13 seasons for the club. He reached 400 Milan matches on 22 August 2009.

    With Milan he won the Coppa Italia 2002–03 and the Champions League 2002–03 and 2006–07. He was also part of the Serie A titles in 2003–04 and 2010–11, the UEFA Super Cup in 2003 and 2007, the Supercoppa Italiana in 2004 and 2011 and the FIFA Club World Cup in 2007.

    He often wore the captain's armband for Milan, and after Paolo Maldini retired he was captain. In May 2012 he met Galliani, and he chose not to renew his contract, which expired on 30 June 2012. In total it was 468 matches and 11 goals for Milan in all competitions. He then played for Sion in 2012–13.

    For Italy he played 73 internationals and scored one goal, against England in November 2000. He won the 2006 World Cup and took part in three World Cups (2002, 2006, 2010), Euro 2004 and 2008 and the 2009 Confederations Cup. As a coach he has led, among others, Pisa, Milan (2017–19), Napoli (cup winners 2020), Valencia, Marseille and Hajduk Split.

    Background

    Ruud Gullit was born Rudi Dil and grew up in the Jordaan area of Amsterdam. His father George Gullit was an immigrant from Suriname and an economics teacher, while his mother Ria Dil worked as a caretaker at the Rijksmuseum. He learned football on the street, first with Meerboys and later at DWS, and took his father's surname because it sounded more like a footballer.

    Gullit played for Haarlem (1979–82), Feyenoord (1982–85) and PSV (1985–87). In the Netherlands he won, among other things, the Player of the Year award in 1984 and 1986. In summer 1987 Milan bought him from PSV for a club-record 13.5 billion lire. His first official Milan match was a Coppa Italia tie against Bari on 23 August 1987, in which he scored.

    Gullit was usually an attacking midfielder or second striker, but could play across the midfield, on both wings and as a libero. He was known for athletic power, balance, dribbling, free-kicks, vision and leadership. Later in his career he played more as a deep-lying playmaker, and he was often plagued by injuries.

    Career

    In his first season, 1987–88, Gullit won Serie A with Milan. The same year he received the Gullit Ballon d'Or for 1987, and he came second in 1988 behind teammate Marco van Basten. In 1988 he also won the European Championship with the Netherlands, heading in the opening goal in the 2–0 win over the Soviet Union in the final.

    Milan won the European Cup (now the Champions League) in 1988–89 and 1989–90, and Gullit was part of both. He also won the UEFA Super Cup and Intercontinental Cup in both 1989 and 1990. In addition came the Supercoppa Italiana, and the Serie A titles in 1991–92 and 1992–93.

    Gullit was loaned to Sampdoria for 1993–94, where he played 31 matches and scored 15 goals, and Sampdoria won the Coppa Italia. He returned to Milan in 1994 and was part of the 1994 Supercoppa Italiana. His last Milan match was Juventus–Milan on 30 October 1994, and on 10 November 1994 he joined Sampdoria permanently. In total it was 171 matches and 56 goals for Milan.

    For the Netherlands Gullit played 66 internationals and scored 17 goals (1981–1994). He ended his career at Chelsea (1995–98) and was player-manager there from 1996, winning the 1997 FA Cup. He then coached Newcastle, Feyenoord, LA Galaxy and Terek Grozny, and has worked as a TV pundit for, among others, the BBC, Sky Sports and beIN Sports.

    Background

    Marco van Basten was born in Utrecht on 31 October 1964 and grew up in the Oog in Al district. He started at the local club EDO aged six and a year later moved on to UVV Utrecht, where he stayed for nine years. After a short stay at Elinkwijk he was signed by Ajax aged 16 ahead of the 1981–82 season.

    At Ajax he developed into one of Europe's most dangerous strikers. Over six years he won three Eredivisie titles, three Dutch cups and the Cup Winners' Cup, and he scored 128 league goals in 133 matches. In 1987 he left the Netherlands to play for AC Milan.

    Van Basten was a central striker. His career was marked by repeated ankle problems, and an injury in December 1992 required another operation. He was never the same after this, and the last years of his playing career were ruined by the injury.

    Career

    Van Basten came to Milan in 1987 and won Serie A straight away in his first season, 1987–88. That same summer the Netherlands became European champions, and he was top scorer at Euro 1988 with five goals and named player of the tournament. He received his first Ballon d'Or for 1988.

    Milan won the European Cup (now the Champions League) two years in a row, 1988–89 and 1989–90, and in both years also the UEFA Super Cup and the Intercontinental Cup (1989 and 1990). Van Basten also won the Ballon d'Or in 1989.

    Milan took further league titles in 1991–92 and 1992–93, and so did the Supercoppa Italiana in 1988 and 1992. In 1992 he received his third Ballon d'Or and was named FIFA World Player of the Year.

    His last match was the 1993 Champions League final, which Milan lost 1–0 to Marseille; he was substituted in the 86th minute. After two years without matches he announced in August 1995 that he was retiring. For the Netherlands he played 58 internationals and scored 24 goals.

    As a manager he led the Netherlands 2004–2008 and Ajax 2008–09, and was later manager of Heerenveen (2012–2014) and AZ (2014). He was assistant coach of the national team 2015–2016, and from 2016 technical director at FIFA. Later he has been a TV analyst.

    Background

    Andriy Shevchenko was born on 29 September 1976 in Dvirkivshchyna in Soviet Ukraine. The family moved to Kyiv's Obolon district in 1979. At the age of nine, in 1986, he joined Dynamo Kyiv's football section. His group was temporarily evacuated after the Chernobyl disaster.

    He boxed in a junior league before choosing football. He failed a dribbling test for a specialist school, but was noticed by a Dynamo scout at a youth tournament. That took him into Dynamo Kyiv, where he turned professional.

    In 1999 he moved from Dynamo Kyiv to Milan for a transfer fee of 25 million dollars, which was a record at the time. At Milan he became known as a pure goalscorer.

    Career

    Already in his first season, 1999–2000, Shevchenko was Serie A top scorer with 24 goals in 32 matches. He finished third in the Ballon d'Or voting in both 1999 and 2000. Injuries meant he scored only five league goals in 24 matches in 2001–02.

    In 2002–03 Milan won the Champions League after a final against Juventus, decided on penalties, and Shevchenko scored his penalty. The same season the club won the Coppa Italia, and in 2003 the UEFA Super Cup.

    In 2003–04 Milan won Serie A, and Shevchenko was again top scorer with 24 goals in 32 matches. The club also won the Supercoppa Italiana in 2004, and that same year he was named Ballon d'Or winner. In the 2004–05 Champions League final Milan lost to Liverpool on penalties, and he missed his penalty. In 2005–06 he scored 19 league goals and was Champions League top scorer with nine goals.

    He turned down a new contract and moved to Chelsea for 44 million euros. Milan brought him back on loan for 2008–09, but he scored no league goals, and he scored two goals in 26 matches in total. For Ukraine he played 111 internationals and scored 48 goals.

    He retired in 2012 to stand in the parliamentary election, but his party got 1.58 per cent and no seats. He was Ukraine's national team manager from 2016 to 2021 and took the team to the quarter-final of Euro 2020. He coached Genoa from November 2021 to January 2022, and became president of the Ukrainian football federation in January 2024.

    Background

    Ricardo Izecson dos Santos Leite was born on 22 April 1982 in Gama in the Federal District of Brazil. The family was well off, which meant he could concentrate on school and football. When he was seven, the family moved to São Paulo. The nickname Kaká comes from his brother Digão having trouble saying "Ricardo".

    Through a local youth club he was spotted by São Paulo FC, who offered him a place at the academy. Aged 18 he suffered a broken back in a swimming pool accident, but made a full recovery. He played for São Paulo 2000–2003 and scored 47 goals in 125 matches.

    In 2003 Milan, then European champions, bought him for a reported fee of 8.5 million euros. He was an attacking midfielder and became a regular within a month.

    Career

    In his debut season 2003–04 Milan won Serie A, and in 2004 also the Supercoppa Italiana. The following season, 2004–05, the team lost the Champions League final to Liverpool on penalties, and Kaká was named UEFA Club Midfielder of the Year.

    The big breakthrough came in 2006–07, when Milan won the Champions League. Kaká scored ten goals in the tournament. That same year the club won the UEFA Super Cup and the Club World Cup, and he was named both Ballon d'Or winner and FIFA World Player for 2007.

    In 2007–08 he scored 15 league goals, and in 2008–09 16 goals in total. Milan finished third in the league, and in the summer of 2009 he left the club for Real Madrid for 67 million euros. In his first spell he played 270 matches and scored 95 goals.

    After four years in Madrid, marked by injuries, he returned to Milan in 2013 and played 37 matches with nine goals in 2013–14, wearing shirt number 22. He then played for Orlando City in the USA, and with Brazil he won the 2002 World Cup and the Confederations Cup in 2005 and 2009; 92 internationals and 29 goals in total.

    He retired on 17 December 2017. He has been an ambassador for the UN World Food Programme since 2004. Milan offered him a position as sporting director.
