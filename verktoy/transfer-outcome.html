Profit/loss Income statement

The transfer fee does not become a cost on the day of purchase. It is spread as amortisation over the contract. Wages come on top, every year.

    Balance sheet and cash Assets and debt

    Here the purchase happens at once: money goes out, and Milan own a new asset. The result is unchanged on the day of purchase.

      Year by year

      Amortisation is the same every year, and the book value on the balance sheet falls to zero. The charge to the result is amortisation + wages.

      Squad cost against the FFP limit of 70%

      Sales: This example leaves out sales. If Milan sell a player for more than his book value, the difference is called a plusvalenza. It is income in the result straight away, while the cash (the full sale price) ends up on the balance sheet. Simplified model, not an official FFP calculation. Squad cost = (wages + amortisation) ÷ revenue. The same 70% limit applies from 2026/27 under UEFA's squad cost rule and the Italian federation's «costo del lavoro allargato». The two rules do not measure things in quite the same way, and we have not publicly reviewed the details of the Italian one. The starting point is rough figures: revenue €464.6m (2025/26, club accounts), wages for the whole squad €103.1m gross (estimate from Calcio e Finanza, September 2026), amortisation €120m (2024/25) and result −€24.0m (estimate for 2025/26). Coaches' wages and agent fees are not included because we lack figures. New players can bring new revenue (sponsors, sporting success), but that is not counted. The club's own figures for 2026/27 have not been published.
