History

Timeline

Milan's history from 1899 to the present day. Drag the timeline or choose a period, and read the story of each era below.

18992026
  1. Foundation and the first titles1899

    The club is founded

    Milan Foot-Ball and Cricket Club was founded in December 1899 by a group of Englishmen and Italians. Alfred Edwards became the first president, while Herbert Kilpin led the football team. The club's first meeting place was a wine bar in central Milan.

  2. 1899

    Red and black

    Kilpin chose the colours red and black. Red was to stand for fire and black for the fear the team would strike into their opponents. The nickname «il Diavolo», the devil, dates from this time.

  3. 1900

    Joining the federation

    Just a month after the club was founded, Edwards registered it with the Italian Football Federation. That meant Milan could compete for the Italian championship. The team played its first matches on a basic pitch in Piazza Doria.

  4. 1901

    First scudetto

    Milan won their first Italian title, breaking Genoa's dominance. Genoa had won every championship until then. Kilpin captained the team that wrote the club's first chapter in the history books.

  5. 1906

    Second championship

    After five years without a title, Milan were once again the best in Italy. The team had a stronger core of regulars, and interest in football was growing fast in the city.

  6. 1907

    Third championship

    Milan defended the title and won their third championship. Nobody knew it then, but it would be 44 years before the club won the league again.

  7. 1908

    The split that created Inter

    A dispute over the use of foreign players led a group of members to break away. They founded Football Club Internazionale in March 1908. Ever since, Milan has had two big clubs, and the derby between them has become one of the world's biggest city rivalries.

  8. 1909

    Pirelli becomes president

    Industrialist Piero Pirelli took over as president. He remained in the post for almost 20 years and had a major impact on the club, not least because he was later behind the construction of San Siro.

  9. 1915

    War halts football

    When Italy entered the First World War in 1915, the national championship was suspended. Many players were called up as soldiers, and it took several years before football was back to normal.

  10. 1919

    New name

    After the war the club changed its name to Milan Football Club. Cricket had long since gone, and football had become the club's only sport.

  11. San Siro and the interwar years1926

    San Siro opens

    The stadium, built on the initiative of president Piero Pirelli, was inaugurated on 19 September 1926. It took around 13 months to build. Inter won the opening match 6–3 against Milan in front of around 35,000 spectators.

  12. 1926

    First competitive match

    The first league match at the new stadium was played in October 1926. Milan lost 2–1 to Sampierdarenese, a club from Genoa. It was a difficult start to life in their new home.

  13. 1928

    Pirelli steps down

    Piero Pirelli stepped down as president after almost 20 years. He left behind a stadium and a solid club, but no new titles.

  14. 1929

    Serie A becomes a single league

    From the 1929–30 season the Italian championship was played as a single national league in which everyone played everyone. Milan were there from the start, and the league took on the format it has today.

  15. 1935

    The city takes over San Siro

    The City of Milan became the owner of the stadium and expanded it so that the stands went all the way round the pitch. Capacity increased to around 55,000.

  16. 1935–1939

    Four semi-finals in a row

    Milan reached the Coppa Italia semi-finals four seasons in a row, but never quite went all the way. In the league, third place was the best finish of the period.

  17. 1936

    Boffi arrives

    Striker Aldo Boffi joined Milan and quickly became the team's leading goalscorer. He is still among the top five scorers in the club's history.

  18. 1938

    Mitropa Cup

    Milan took part in the Mitropa Cup, the major club competition for teams from Central Europe before the war. It is regarded as a forerunner of today's European competitions.

  19. 1939

    Top scorer Boffi

    Aldo Boffi was Serie A top scorer in 1938–39, shared with Hector Puricelli. He won the same title again in 1939–40 and 1941–42.

  20. 1939

    Forced to take an Italian name

    Under Fascism English names were frowned upon, and the club had to change its name to Associazione Calcio Milano. The English «Milan» only returned after the war.

  21. Gre-No-Li and the comeback1940

    Meazza in red and black

    Giuseppe Meazza, Inter's biggest star, switched to Milan. He played for the club for two seasons. Today San Siro officially bears his name.

  22. 1942

    Boffi's third top scorer title

    Aldo Boffi was Serie A top scorer for the third time in 1941–42. Shortly afterwards the war halted the regular league.

  23. 1945

    Back to “Milan”

    After the Second World War the club took the name Associazione Calcio Milan. The English «Milan» was back, and so it has remained ever since.

  24. 1949

    Nordahl arrives

    Swedish striker Gunnar Nordahl joined Milan in January 1949. He had won Olympic gold with Sweden the year before. Nordahl scored 16 goals in his first 15 matches.

  25. 1949

    Gre-No-Li is complete

    In the summer of 1949 the Swedes Gunnar Gren and Nils Liedholm also arrived. The trio were nicknamed Gre-No-Li after the first letters of their surnames. Liedholm stayed at the club for many years, later also as coach.

  26. 1950

    7–1 in Turin

    In February 1950 Milan crushed Juventus 7–1 away from home. The team scored over a hundred league goals that season, but Juventus still won the title.

  27. 1951

    First scudetto since 1907

    Milan won the 1950–51 title, their first in 44 years. The coach was the Hungarian Lajos Czeizler, and Nordahl was top scorer. The wait was finally over.

  28. 1951

    Latin Cup

    In June 1951 Milan beat French side Lille 5–0 in the Latin Cup final at San Siro. The tournament brought together the champions of Italy, Spain, Portugal and France and was a forerunner of the European Cup.

  29. 1954

    Schiaffino arrives

    The Uruguayan Juan Alberto Schiaffino, who had won the 1950 World Cup, joined Milan. He became one of the team's most important players for the rest of the decade.

  30. 1955

    Scudetto number five

    Milan won Serie A in 1954–55. Nordahl was top scorer for the fifth and final time.

  31. 1956

    Another Latin Cup

    Milan won the Latin Cup for the second time after a 3–1 win over Athletic Bilbao in the final in Milan. The club had now won the prestigious tournament twice in five years.

  32. 1957

    Scudetto number six

    Milan won Serie A in 1956–57 and thereby qualified for the European Cup for the second time. This time the team would go all the way to the final.

  33. 1958

    First European Cup final

    Milan reached the European Cup final in Brussels, but lost 3–2 after extra time to Real Madrid. Milan led twice, but Paco Gento settled it late in extra time.

  34. 1959

    Scudetto number seven

    Milan won Serie A in 1958–59 with Liedholm, Schiaffino and the young José Altafini among others in the side. The decade thus ended with four titles.

  35. Rocco, Rivera and Europe1960

    Rivera arrives

    Gianni Rivera joined Milan from Alessandria as a teenager. He played for the club for 19 seasons and is still among the players with the most appearances and goals for Milan.

  36. 1962

    Scudetto under Rocco

    Nereo Rocco came to Milan in 1961 and led the team to the title in his very first season. Young Rivera and goalscorer Altafini were key figures. Rocco emphasised a solid defence and quick attacks.

  37. 1963

    First European Cup title

    On 22 May 1963 Milan beat Benfica 2–1 at Wembley and became the first Italian winners of the European Cup. Eusébio gave Benfica the lead, but Altafini scored twice from Rivera's passes. Altafini scored 14 goals in the competition that season. Captain Cesare Maldini, father of Paolo, lifted the trophy.

  38. 1963

    Defeat to Pelé's Santos

    As European champions, Milan faced Brazil's Santos in the Intercontinental Cup. Milan won at home but lost away, and Santos won the deciding play-off in Rio de Janeiro. After the season Rocco left the club for a spell.

  39. 1967

    First Coppa Italia

    Milan won the Coppa Italia for the first time. That same year Nereo Rocco returned as coach, laying the foundations for a new golden age.

  40. 1968

    Scudetto and the Cup Winners' Cup

    Milan won Serie A in 1967–68 and beat Hamburger SV 2–0 in the Cup Winners' Cup final in Rotterdam. Swede Kurt Hamrin scored both goals.

  41. 1969

    Second European Cup

    On 28 May 1969 Milan crushed Ajax 4–1 in the European Cup final in Madrid. Pierino Prati scored three of the goals. On the way to the final, Milan knocked out the reigning champions Manchester United.

  42. 1969

    Intercontinental Cup and the Ballon d'Or

    Milan beat Estudiantes 3–0 at home and lost 2–1 in an extremely violent second leg in Buenos Aires, which was enough for victory on aggregate. That same year Gianni Rivera was named Europe's best player with the Ballon d'Or.

  43. 1972

    Another cup win

    Milan won the Coppa Italia for the second time. The club was often near the top of the league at this time, but had more success in the cup competitions.

  44. 1973

    The Cup Winners' Cup and «fatal Verona»

    Milan beat Leeds United 1–0 in the Cup Winners' Cup final in Thessaloniki and also won the Coppa Italia. Four days after the final the team lost 5–3 to Verona on the final matchday, and Juventus took the title. The defeat has become a byword among Milan fans.

  45. 1977

    Cup final against Inter

    Milan won the Coppa Italia for the fourth time after beating Inter 2–0 in the final. Beating their city rivals in a final made the win extra sweet for the fans.

  46. 1979

    Tenth Scudetto and the star

    With Nils Liedholm as coach, Milan won their tenth title in 1978–79 and earned the star on the shirt. Rivera retired after the season and moved into the club's management.

  47. From scandal to the top of the world1980

    Totonero relegation

    On 23 March 1980 several Serie A players were arrested after the league matches, among them Milan goalkeeper Enrico Albertosi and president Felice Colombo. The case concerned illegal betting and match-fixing. Milan were forcibly relegated to Serie B, and Colombo was banned from football.

  48. 1981–1983

    Down and back up again

    Milan won Serie B in 1980–81 and came straight back up, but were relegated again in 1981–82, this time because of poor results on the pitch. The team also won Serie B in 1982–83 and were quickly back in Serie A.

  49. 1985

    Maldini makes his debut

    Paolo Maldini, son of European Cup-winning captain Cesare, made his Milan debut in January 1985 aged 16. He spent his entire career at the club and holds the record for most appearances for Milan.

  50. 1986

    Berlusconi buys the club

    Silvio Berlusconi saved Milan from bankruptcy on 20 February 1986. He promised attractive football and that Milan would become the best in the world. The team was presented with a helicopter landing at the Arena Civica, accompanied by Wagner.

  51. 1987

    Sacchi and the Dutchmen

    Berlusconi hired Arrigo Sacchi, who had never played at a high level himself. At the same time Marco van Basten and Ruud Gullit arrived. Gullit won the Ballon d'Or the same year.

  52. 1988

    Sacchi's first scudetto

    Milan won Serie A in 1987–88 ahead of Diego Maradona's Napoli. The decisive match was a 3–2 win in Naples on 1 May 1988. The following summer Frank Rijkaard arrived, and the Dutch trio was complete.

  53. 1989

    The European Cup returns

    Milan crushed Real Madrid 5–0 in the semi-final and beat Steaua Bucureşti 4–0 in the final in Barcelona on 24 May 1989. Gullit and Van Basten scored two goals each. Later that year Milan also won the Intercontinental Cup against Atlético Nacional and the UEFA Super Cup.

  54. 1990

    Defended the European Cup

    Milan beat Benfica 1–0 in the final in Vienna with a goal from Rijkaard and became the last team to retain the European Cup before it became the Champions League. The team again won both the Intercontinental Cup, against Olimpia, and the Super Cup. In the league, Milan lost the title after another bitter defeat to Verona.

  55. 1991

    The night in Marseille

    In the European Cup quarter-final against Marseille, some of the floodlights failed while Milan were 1–0 down. Milan refused to play on, and the match was abandoned. UEFA banned the club from European competition for one year, and Sacchi then left for the national team.

  56. 1992

    Unbeaten champions

    In Fabio Capello's first season, Milan went through the whole of Serie A 1991–92 without a single defeat. The unbeaten run began in May 1991 and lasted until March 1993, a total of 58 league matches. It is still a Serie A record.

  57. 1993

    The run comes to an end

    Parma ended the unbeaten run with a 1–0 win at San Siro on 21 March 1993. Milan still won their second straight title, but lost the Champions League final 1–0 to Marseille in Munich.

  58. 1994

    Barcelona crushed in Athens

    Milan won their third straight title and beat Johan Cruyff's Barcelona 4–0 in the Champions League final in Athens on 18 May 1994. Baresi and Costacurta were suspended, but the defence still kept a clean sheet. Daniele Massaro scored two of the goals.

  59. 1995–1996

    Weah and another title

    Milan reached their third straight Champions League final in 1995, but lost 1–0 to Ajax in Vienna. That same year Liberian George Weah won the Ballon d'Or as a Milan player. With Weah and Roberto Baggio up front, Milan won Serie A in 1995–96, before Capello left for Real Madrid.

  60. 1997

    Baresi retires

    After a difficult season, Franco Baresi ended his career in 1997. The club retired the number 6 shirt in honour of its captain.

  61. 1999

    The centenary title

    Under Alberto Zaccheroni, Milan won Serie A in 1998–99, the club's 16th Scudetto. The team caught Lazio at the end of the season and sealed the title with an away win at Perugia on the final matchday. It was the perfect centenary.

  62. The Ancelotti years and a long decline2001

    The derby that ended 6–0

    In May 2001 Milan won 6–0 away to Inter, one of the biggest derby wins in history. Shevchenko scored two of the goals. The season was otherwise disappointing.

  63. 2001

    Ancelotti becomes head coach

    In November 2001 Carlo Ancelotti took over from Fatih Terim. Around the same time players such as Andrea Pirlo, Filippo Inzaghi and Rui Costa arrived. Alessandro Nesta and Clarence Seedorf followed the next year.

  64. 2003

    Champions League at Old Trafford

    Milan beat Juventus on penalties after a 0–0 draw in the final in Manchester. Shevchenko scored the decisive penalty. On the way, Milan had knocked out Inter in the semi-final. That same spring the team also won the Coppa Italia against Roma.

  65. 2004

    Scudetto number 17

    Ancelotti's Milan won Serie A in 2003–04 with newcomer Kaká from São Paulo as one of the stars. Shevchenko was top scorer and won the Ballon d'Or the same year.

  66. 2005

    The Istanbul nightmare

    Milan led 3–0 at half-time in the Champions League final against Liverpool after goals from Maldini and two from Hernán Crespo. Liverpool equalised with three goals in six minutes and won on penalties. The defeat is regarded as one of the most shocking in the history of the final.

  67. 2006

    Calciopoli punishment

    Milan were docked points in the Calciopoli refereeing scandal, both for 2005–06 and as a starting penalty in 2006–07, reduced from 15 to 8 points on appeal. That same summer Shevchenko was sold to Chelsea.

  68. 2007

    Revenge in Athens

    Milan beat Liverpool 2–1 in the Champions League final on 23 May 2007, with two goals from Filippo Inzaghi. It was the club's seventh European title. The team also won the UEFA Super Cup that autumn.

  69. 2007

    World champions and the Ballon d'Or

    In December Milan beat Boca Juniors 4–2 in the final of the Club World Cup in Japan. Kaká won the Ballon d'Or the same year. Milan thus became the first European club to win the new competition.

  70. 2009

    The end of an era

    Paolo Maldini played his last match for Milan after 25 seasons in the first team. That same summer Ancelotti left for Chelsea and Kaká was sold to Real Madrid.

  71. 2011

    Scudetto number 18

    In autumn 2010 Milan signed Zlatan Ibrahimović from Barcelona and Robinho from Manchester City, and Massimiliano Allegri was the new head coach. Milan sealed the title on 7 May 2011 after a 0–0 draw with Roma. The team also won the Supercoppa Italiana against Inter later that year.

  72. 2012

    The great walkout

    In the summer of 2012 Milan sold both Ibrahimović and Thiago Silva to Paris Saint-Germain. At the same time veterans such as Nesta, Gattuso, Seedorf and Inzaghi left the club. It marked the start of several difficult years.

  73. 2016

    The Supercoppa in Doha

    Under Vincenzo Montella, Milan beat Juventus on penalties in the Supercoppa Italiana in Qatar in December 2016. It was the club's first trophy in five years.

  74. 2017

    End of the Berlusconi era

    On 13 April 2017 Berlusconi sold the club for 740 million euros to a Chinese company led by Li Yonghong. Under Berlusconi, Milan had won 29 trophies in 31 years. The new owners spent heavily on players the following summer.

  75. 2018

    Elliott take over

    The purchase was financed with loans from the investment fund Elliott Management. When Li failed to repay on time, Elliott took over the club in July 2018. Shortly afterwards Paolo Maldini returned as an executive.

  76. 2019

    European ban and Pioli in

    Milan were excluded from the 2019–20 Europa League for breaching UEFA's financial rules. After a poor start to the season, Stefano Pioli was appointed head coach in October 2019.

  77. A new scudetto and a new era2020

    Ibrahimović returns

    Zlatan Ibrahimović returned to Milan in January 2020, aged 38. He became a leader for a young team. After the pandemic break the team was unbeaten for the rest of the season, and Pioli was kept on.

  78. 2021

    Back in the Champions League

    Milan finished second in Serie A in 2020–21. That meant the club was back in the Champions League for the first time since the 2013–14 season.

  79. 2022

    Scudetto number 19

    Stefano Pioli's Milan won Serie A on 22 May 2022 with a club record of 86 points. The team sealed it with a 3–0 win away to Sassuolo on the final matchday. Rafael Leão was named Serie A's player of the season.

  80. 2022

    RedBird the new owner

    The American investment firm RedBird Capital Partners, led by Gerry Cardinale, completed its purchase of Milan on 31 August 2022. The price was around 1.2 billion euros, and Elliott kept a minority stake.

  81. 2023

    Semi-final and Maldini exit

    Milan reached the Champions League semi-finals for the first time since 2007, but lost both legs to Inter. In June 2023 the club removed Paolo Maldini from his role as technical director. Ibrahimović retired the same month.

  82. 2023

    Berlusconi dies

    Silvio Berlusconi died on 12 June 2023. He owned Milan for 31 years, and under him the club won, among other things, five Champions League or European Cup titles and eight league titles.

  83. 2024

    Pioli leaves

    Stefano Pioli left after almost five seasons and was replaced by Paulo Fonseca. Fonseca was sacked in December 2024, and Sérgio Conceição took over on 30 December.

  84. 2025

    Comeback in Riyadh

    In January 2025 Milan won the Supercoppa Italiana in Saudi Arabia. In the final they were 2–0 down against Inter, but won 3–2 with goals from Theo Hernández, Christian Pulisic and Tammy Abraham right at the death. It was only Conceição's second match in charge.

  85. 2025

    Allegri returns

    After a cup final defeat to Bologna and an 8th-place league finish, Milan were out of Europe for the first time since 2016. On 30 May 2025 Massimiliano Allegri was presented as head coach for the second time, eleven years after he left the club. Igli Tare became the new sporting director. Luka Modrić was among the players who arrived that summer.

  86. 2025

    Buys San Siro

    Milan city council approved the sale on 30 September 2025, and in November Milan and Inter bought the stadium and the surrounding area for 197 million euros. The plan is to build a new stadium.

  87. 2026

    Fifth place and a change of coach

    With Allegri back, Milan finished fifth in 2025–26 with 70 points and qualified for the Europa League. A 2–1 home defeat to Cagliari on the final matchday cost them a Champions League place. Allegri was sacked the next day.

  88. 2026

    Amorim new head coach

    On 16 June 2026 Portugal's Rúben Amorim was presented as the new head coach. He had previously enjoyed great success with Sporting in Lisbon, but a difficult spell at Manchester United.

  89. 2026

    Franco Baresi has died

    Club legend and captain Franco Baresi died aged 66, and Milan announced his death on 31 July 2026. He spent his entire career at Milan, from 1977 to 1997, and the number 6 shirt is retired.

  90. 2026

    San Siro at 100

    The stadium turned 100 on 19 September 2026. Milan marked the anniversary with a ceremony before the home match against Lecce. The celebrations were coloured by the fact that the stadium is due to be replaced by a new one.

1899–1919

Foundation and the first titles

Milan were founded by Englishmen and Italians in December 1899 and won three Italian titles before the First World War. At the same time an internal dispute led to the birth of city rivals Inter.

Milan around 1900 was a fast-growing city, with factories, trade and many foreigners. Among them was a group of Englishmen who had brought football with them from home. In December 1899 they joined Italian friends and founded Milan Foot-Ball and Cricket Club, with Alfred Edwards as president and Herbert Kilpin leading the team on the pitch. The name was written in English, «Milan», and so it remains.

Herbert Kilpin was the heart of the early club. He was captain, coach and driving force, and he is credited with choosing the colours. According to the story, he said the team would be a team of devils, red as fire and black as the fear they would strike into their opponents. That is why fans still call Milan «il Diavolo», the devil.

Success came quickly. In 1901 Milan broke Genoa's grip on the Italian championship, and in 1906 and 1907 two more titles followed. Football was then a sport for the few, with small stands and matches on basic pitches, but Milan soon became a name across the country.

Then came the split. In 1908 there was a dispute within the club over how many foreign players the team should be allowed to use, and a group of members left Milan and founded Internazionale. It was the start of the Derby della Madonnina, one of the world's most famous city derbies. After the first three titles came a long barren spell, and the First World War put football on hold for several years.

1920–1939

San Siro and the interwar years

Milan got their own stadium in 1926, but won no titles in the interwar years. The period was marked by evenly matched teams, Fascist interference and goal king Aldo Boffi.

The interwar years were lean for Milan on the pitch. The club was a fixture in the top division, but had to watch Inter, Juventus, Bologna and others collect the titles. For supporters these were years of hope that was rarely fulfilled.

The biggest event happened off the pitch. President Piero Pirelli had a stadium of the club's own built in the San Siro district, and in September 1926 it was opened with a derby against Inter. Milan lost 6–3, but gained a home that would become one of the most famous football stadiums in the world. In 1935 the city council took over ownership and expanded the stands.

From 1929 Serie A was played as a single national league, as we know it today. At the same time the Fascist regime interfered more and more in sport. English names were unpopular with those in power, and in 1939 the club had to take the Italian name Associazione Calcio Milano.

Towards the end of the 1930s the fans still had something to cheer about. Striker Aldo Boffi joined the club in 1936 and became Serie A top scorer. Milan reached the Coppa Italia semi-finals four years in a row and got a taste of the Mitropa Cup, the great competition of the era for clubs from Central Europe.

1940–1959

Gre-No-Li and the comeback

After the war Milan became a top side again with the Swedish trio Gren, Nordahl and Liedholm. The club won four titles in the 1950s and played its first European Cup final.

The war years were hard for Milan and for football. The city was bombed, the league was interrupted, and the club had to use an imposed Italian name. One bright spot was that Inter legend Giuseppe Meazza played for Milan for two seasons around 1940. After the liberation in 1945 the club got the name Milan back.

The turning point came in 1949. Sweden had won the Olympics in London the year before, and Milan first signed striker Gunnar Nordahl and then Gunnar Gren and Nils Liedholm. The trio were nicknamed Gre-No-Li. Nordahl was a powerful goal machine, Gren a clever playmaker and Liedholm an elegant midfielder who almost never misplaced a pass. In their very first season the team scored over a hundred league goals and won 7–1 away to Juventus.

In 1951 the Scudetto finally returned after 44 years, and that same summer Milan won the Latin Cup, a tournament for the champions of Italy, Spain, Portugal and France. Nordahl was Serie A top scorer five times and is still the club's all-time leading scorer. Later came the Uruguayan Juan Alberto Schiaffino, a world champion from 1950, and the Brazilian José Altafini.

Milan also won the league in 1955, 1957 and 1959 and were now one of the best teams in Europe. In 1958 the club reached its first European Cup final, but lost 3–2 after extra time to Real Madrid. For supporters this decade was a rebirth. Milan were no longer the team watching others win, but the team everyone wanted to beat.

1960–1979

Rocco, Rivera and Europe

Under Nereo Rocco and with Gianni Rivera as the star, Milan became the best in Europe for the first time. The period ended with the tenth title and the star on the shirt.

In 1960 a teenager from Alessandria arrived at Milan. Gianni Rivera was slight and elegant, but had an eye for a pass that few others possessed. He became the club's great star for almost 20 years and earned the nickname «il Golden Boy». In 1969 he became the first Italian-born player to win the Ballon d'Or.

The coach who gave Rivera his framework was Nereo Rocco from Trieste. Rocco was known for a cautious, well-organised defensive game, in which the team's first priority was to avoid conceding and then strike on the counter. It was effective. Milan won the league in 1962, and the following year the team beat Benfica at Wembley and became the first Italian winners of the European Cup. Brazilian José Altafini scored both goals in the final from Rivera's passes.

Rocco left for a spell, but returned in 1967 and gave the club a new golden era. Milan won the league and the Cup Winners' Cup in 1968, and in 1969 Ajax, with a young Johan Cruyff, were crushed 4–1 in the European Cup final in Madrid. That autumn Milan beat Argentina's Estudiantes in the Intercontinental Cup, in a second leg known as one of the most brutal in history.

The 1970s were more uneven. Milan won the Coppa Italia several times and the Cup Winners' Cup again in 1973, but that same year lost the title on the final matchday with a 5–3 defeat away to Verona. Fans still call that defeat «fatal Verona». Meanwhile, Inter and Juventus were often stronger in the league.

The period still had a fine ending. With Nils Liedholm as coach, Milan won their tenth title in 1979 and earned the right to wear a star above the club crest. Rivera retired after the season. An era was over, and nobody suspected how dark the next few years would be.

1980–1999

From scandal to the top of the world

The decade began with enforced relegation and near bankruptcy, but Berlusconi, Sacchi and Capello turned Milan into one of the greatest club sides of all time.

Just one year after the star, Milan fell all the way. In March 1980 several players and president Felice Colombo were arrested in the Totonero scandal over illegal betting and match-fixing. Milan were forcibly relegated to Serie B, and the president was banned from football. The team quickly came back, but went down again on sporting merit in 1982, the same summer Italy became world champions.

By the mid-1980s the club was almost broke. Bright spots were few, but important. Young Franco Baresi had become captain, and in January 1985 a 16-year-old left-back named Paolo Maldini made his debut. In February 1986 media magnate Silvio Berlusconi bought the club and saved it from bankruptcy. He presented the team by landing in a helicopter at the Arena Civica while Wagner's «Ride of the Valkyries» boomed around the stadium.

Berlusconi took a chance on an almost unknown coach from Parma, Arrigo Sacchi, and signed the Dutchmen Marco van Basten, Ruud Gullit and later Frank Rijkaard. Sacchi wanted the whole team to attack and defend together, with a high defensive line, a compact shape and constant pressure on the ball carrier. The defence of Baresi, Maldini, Tassotti and Costacurta became legendary. Milan won the league in 1988 and the European Cup in 1989 and 1990, and many consider this one of the greatest club sides of all time.

When Sacchi became national team manager in 1991, Fabio Capello took over. He was more pragmatic, but the results were even more impressive. Milan went through an entire league season unbeaten, set a record of 58 league matches in a row without defeat and won three straight titles. The high point came in Athens in 1994, when Barcelona were beaten 4–0 in the Champions League final.

Towards the end of the decade, the cracks began to show. Van Basten had to retire because of an ankle injury, Baresi stepped down in 1997, and two seasons ended far down the table. But in 1999, exactly one hundred years after the club was founded, Alberto Zaccheroni's Milan won the title after a strong finish to the season. For the fans it was the perfect centenary gift.

2000–2019

The Ancelotti years and a long decline

With Carlo Ancelotti, Milan won two Champions League titles, but also lost the most dramatic final in history. The club then entered a difficult period of ownership changes and poor results.

The new millennium began with Andriy Shevchenko leading the line. The Ukrainian arrived from Dynamo Kyiv in 1999 and quickly became one of Europe's best strikers. After a couple of turbulent seasons, Berlusconi and director Adriano Galliani brought in former Milan player Carlo Ancelotti as head coach in November 2001.

Ancelotti built a team around technically gifted midfielders. Andrea Pirlo was moved back in front of the defence and dictated play from there, with Gennaro Gattuso as his tireless partner and Clarence Seedorf and later Kaká ahead of them. Behind them stood Maldini, Alessandro Nesta and Cafu, and up front Shevchenko and Filippo Inzaghi scored the goals. Milan won the Champions League in 2003 and the league in 2004.

No Milan fan will ever forget that night in Istanbul in 2005. The team led 3–0 at half-time in the final against Liverpool, but conceded three goals in six minutes and lost on penalties. The following year the club was dragged into the Calciopoli refereeing scandal and docked points. Even so, the team got its revenge in Athens in 2007, when Liverpool were beaten 2–1. Kaká won the Ballon d'Or, and Milan became club world champions.

After 2009 the big names disappeared one by one. Ancelotti left, Maldini retired and Kaká was sold. Under Massimiliano Allegri and with Zlatan Ibrahimović up front, Milan still won the league in 2011, but in the summer of 2012 both Ibrahimović and Thiago Silva were sold, and many veterans left at the same time. The club struggled financially and changed coaches often.

Berlusconi sold the club in 2017 after more than 30 years. The new Chinese owners spent heavily, but the purchase was financed with loans, and the following year the investment fund Elliott took over. For supporters this was a decade of frustration, with Milan often absent from the Champions League and forced to watch Juventus win title after title.

2020–2026

A new scudetto and a new era

Milan won their 19th title in 2022, but recent seasons have been marked by a change of ownership, coaching changes, the stadium question and grief.

When Zlatan Ibrahimović returned in January 2020, Milan were a team without confidence. Shortly afterwards the pandemic halted football, and matches were played in empty stadiums. But Stefano Pioli's young side found their form after the break, and in 2021 Milan were back in the Champions League for the first time in seven years.

The 2021–22 season was a fairytale. With goalkeeper Mike Maignan, centre-back Fikayo Tomori, full-back Theo Hernández, Sandro Tonali in midfield and Rafael Leão as the big weapon up front, Milan caught Inter at the end and won their 19th title. The team was young, fast and hungry, and for many younger supporters it was the first time they had seen the club win the league.

That same summer the American investment firm RedBird bought the club from Elliott. The following year Milan reached the Champions League semi-finals, but lost to Inter. Shortly afterwards Paolo Maldini was removed from the management, which provoked strong reactions among the fans. In June 2023 Silvio Berlusconi, the man behind the club's greatest era, also died.

After Pioli left in 2024, a turbulent period followed. Paulo Fonseca was sacked after half a season, and Sérgio Conceição won the Supercoppa against Inter with a dramatic comeback, but the team finished 8th and missed out on Europe. Massimiliano Allegri returned in 2025, but fifth place in 2026 was not enough, and he was sacked the day after the final matchday.

The summer and autumn of 2026 have been marked by both a fresh start and grief. Portugal's Rúben Amorim became the new head coach, and the club, together with Inter, has bought San Siro with plans for a new stadium. In July club legend Franco Baresi died, and in September San Siro turned 100. For supporters this is a time to remember the history and hope that the next great era is near.
